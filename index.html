How the Scanner Works

A purely price-based screen designed to identify Stage 1 basing setups — assets that have completed their decline phase and entered an accumulation range. Three conditions, all mechanical, no fundamentals.

C1Drawdown ≥ Threshold from 3Y High

Computes drawdown = (current_price / max(price, 756d)) − 1. A meaningful punishment threshold — 30% by default — filters for assets where most discretionary holders have either capitulated or rationalized losses. Below 30%, natural sellers thin out.

C2No New 252-Day Low for ≥ N Days

Counts trading days since the last visit to the rolling 252-day low. The behavioral signal of seller exhaustion. If price hasn't broken to a new annual low in 90+ days, every weak hand has already capitulated.

C36-Month Range < X% of Average Price

Computes (high_6m − low_6m) / avg_6m. Volatility compression / accumulation signal. When something falls 30% but trades in a tight 15% range for 6 months, supply and demand have reached temporary equilibrium — the Wyckoff accumulation zone.

Composite Score — cumulative chain

The conditions form a sequence, not a checklist: C2 only counts if C1 holds, and C3 only counts if C1 and C2 both hold. The chain mirrors the Stage 1 narrative — first capitulation (C1), then seller exhaustion (C2), then quiet accumulation (C3). A tight range without a prior crash is consolidation, not a contrarian base, so it scores 0. Possible scores are therefore 0 (no capitulation), 1 (crashed, still making lows), 2 (crashed and lows exhausted), 3 (full setup). When a condition's raw threshold is met but an earlier link is missing, the detail view marks it "gated".

Caveat

This screen identifies candidates, not entries. A momentum-first investor should pair this with a trend-confirmation trigger (price > 50DMA, 50DMA flattening) before sizing a position. Without that, you'll catch falling knives — a stock down 30% can easily go down another 50%.
